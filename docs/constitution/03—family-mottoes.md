# The Birch Family Constitution

# The Family Mottoes

Every family has sayings. Most of them arrive by accident — a phrase somebody
used once that got repeated until it stuck, and nobody can quite remember who
said it first. We have plenty of those, and they are part of what makes this
house ours.

These two are different, because we chose them on purpose. They are simple
enough that our youngest can repeat them and deep enough that we expect to still
be learning from them when we are old. When we are uncertain, they remind us
where to look; when we disagree, they remind us how to act. They are not slogans
to be printed on a wall and forgotten. They are invitations, and they are meant
to be answered.

---

## Love Like Jesus.

Every person we meet is a child of God, carrying burdens we cannot see, and
worthy of kindness, dignity, patience, and respect on that basis alone — not on
the basis of how they have treated us lately.

Jesus Christ never confused love with weakness. He loved perfectly while also
teaching truth perfectly. He forgave without excusing sin, welcomed the outcast
without lowering God's standards, and showed compassion without surrendering a
single conviction. That combination is much harder than either half on its own,
and it is the thing we are actually aiming at. Loving like Jesus is not the same
as being nice. Nice avoids the hard conversation; love has it gently.

In practice it means choosing charity when pride would be easier, forgiving when
resentment feels earned, listening before speaking, and seeking to understand
somebody before judging them. It means noticing the lonely, defending the
vulnerable, welcoming the stranger, and serving quietly without waiting to be
thanked for it. Most of all it means remembering that the person always comes
before the problem.

We will not always agree with each other in this family. We will disappoint each
other, and occasionally we will hurt each other. When those moments come — and
they will — this motto is here to remind us that winning an argument has never
once been more important than the person standing in front of us. Love is not
mainly a feeling we wait to have. It is something we choose, on purpose, on
days when we do not feel like it.

---

## Think Celestial.

Life is a long series of decisions, a few of which shape generations and most of
which feel like nothing at the time. The world has a standard question for all
of them: *what do you want right now?* The gospel asks a better one: *who are you
becoming?*

To think celestial is to remember that this life is part of something much
larger, and to make today's decisions with eternity somewhere in the frame. It
means holding on to the idea that character matters more than comfort, that
covenants matter more than convenience, and that becoming matters more than
appearing. Those three trades are, between them, most of what a hard decision
actually consists of.

It is not an abstraction. Thinking celestially changes how we spend our time and
our money, how we treat the people who live with us, how we respond when we are
offended, how we use our devices, how we speak when nobody is listening, and who
we choose as friends. When we remember who we are, whose we are, and where we
hope to end up, a surprising number of difficult decisions turn out to have
obvious answers we were avoiding.

---

The two belong together, and neither works properly alone. *Love Like Jesus*
answers the question of how to treat the person in front of us. *Think Celestial*
answers the question of what to choose when the choice is ours. A family that
only had the first would be warm and directionless; a family that only had the
second would be principled and cold. We would like to be neither.

May these stop being phrases we repeat and become the way we live.
