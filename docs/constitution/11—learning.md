# The Birch Family Constitution

# Learning

> "The glory of God is intelligence, or, in other words, light and truth."
>
> — Doctrine and Covenants 93:36

One of God's standing invitations is simply to come and learn. The scriptures are
full of people who received something remarkable because they were willing to ask
for it: Nephi asked how to build a ship, Enos asked how to be forgiven, the
brother of Jared asked how to bring light into the barges, and Joseph Smith asked
which church he should join. Again and again, revelation started with somebody
humble enough to admit they did not know.

We hope to be lifelong learners in this family — not principally because learning
leads to good careers and interesting conversations, although it usually does,
but because every truth anybody discovers is part of God's truth. Whether we are
studying scripture, mathematics, literature, science, history, music, art,
engineering, or the way weather works over the Rexburg valley, we are finding out
something about the God who made all of it. Truth never competes with truth. It
all belongs to Him in the end.

---

We want this to be a home where questions are welcome. Some have simple answers
and some take years of study, prayer, and patience, and both kinds are worth
asking. A question is not evidence of weak faith; frequently it is evidence of
faith that has started growing and needs more room. The Lord rarely condemns
sincere seekers — He tells them to ask, seek, knock, study, ponder, and pray,
which is a fairly clear indication of how He feels about the practice.

Our children should never be afraid to ask a hard question, including the ones
that make the adults in the room uncomfortable. Truth can withstand honest
investigation; that is more or less what makes it truth. And when we do not know
an answer, the right response is "I don't know yet" rather than something
confident and invented. Those three words are not a failure. They are where more
learning starts.

---

Learning requires humility, and humility gets harder with age. The longer we
live, the easier it becomes to assume we already understand enough, and pride
quietly closes doors that curiosity would have walked straight through. A humble
person stays teachable: listening more carefully, reading more thoughtfully,
changing their mind when the evidence asks them to, admitting mistakes, and
receiving correction without treating it as an attack. The Lord can teach a
humble heart almost anything. A proud one struggles with simple things because it
is fully occupied defending what it already believes.

---

Not all learning happens in books. Some of the most durable lessons arrive
through experience — failure teaches resilience, service teaches compassion,
marriage teaches sacrifice, parenthood teaches patience, work teaches
responsibility, and repentance teaches mercy. Trials frequently turn out to have
been classrooms that no university could have built. When life gets difficult, we
hope to ask not only "why is this happening" but also "what is the Lord trying to
teach me," which is a question that turns hardship into discipleship without
making it hurt any less.

Learning should also lead somewhere. Knowledge that changes nothing has not
finished being learned. Jesus concluded the Sermon on the Mount by comparing two
builders who both heard His words, only one of whom did anything about them.
Wisdom is not measured by how much a person knows but by how faithfully they live
what they know. Every scripture studied should shape a choice, and every lesson
ought to leave us a little kinder and a little more like Jesus Christ than it
found us.

---

Books have always been treasured here. Through them we meet people from other
centuries and cultures, travel to places we will never see, wrestle with ideas we
would not have thought of, and occasionally have our minds changed by somebody
who has been dead for four hundred years. We hope our children become readers not
because reading is assigned but because books become lifelong friends. Some
entertain, some educate, and a few quietly rearrange the person reading them —
those are the ones worth going back to. The scriptures manage all three.

---

Technology has made knowledge more available than at any point in history, and
with a few taps we can learn almost anything. That is a genuine blessing and it
comes with a corresponding responsibility, because not everything popular is
true and not everything stated confidently is correct.

We would like to become careful thinkers: people who verify before repeating,
read past the headline, look for reliable sources, and understand that search
engines, social media, artificial intelligence, and even voices we trust can all
be wrong. Truth deserves more attention than it usually gets. As disciples we
pursue it with both faith and reason, welcoming evidence and valuing revelation,
on the understanding that these two have never actually been in competition.

---

Some learning takes years, and patience is part of an education. There are
stretches when progress feels imperceptible; concepts that seem impossible one
year are obvious the next, and skills that felt awkward become second nature
through nothing more interesting than practice. The Lord rarely rushes growth,
and neither should we. We hope our children learn to celebrate progress instead
of demanding perfection from themselves, because every honest effort counts —
every page read, every problem solved, every question asked, every instrument
practiced — and small efforts compound into abilities that look, from the
outside, like talent.

---

Perhaps the most valuable thing anybody can learn is how to recognize the voice
of the Holy Ghost. There is no shortage of intelligent people in the world.
There are fewer who consistently seek and recognize revelation. Education
enriches a life and revelation gives it direction, and we hope this family holds
on to both — thoughtful scholars and humble disciples, people who love books,
love truth, and love God enough to keep learning for the rest of their lives. No
matter how much any of us learns here, there will always be more, which is one of
the more joyful promises attached to eternity.

> "Seek learning, even by study and also by faith."
>
> — Doctrine and Covenants 88:118
