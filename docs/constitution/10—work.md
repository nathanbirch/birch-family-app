# The Birch Family Constitution

# Work

> "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread."
>
> — Genesis 3:19

Work has been part of God's plan from the beginning. Before there were careers,
salaries, or promotions there was meaningful work: Adam and Eve were given a
garden to tend, Noah built an ark, Nephi built a ship, and the Savior spent most
of His mortal life working with His hands before He ever began to preach.

Work is not simply how we pay for things. It is one of the main ways God shapes a
character, which is why this family treats it as honorable rather than as the
tax we pay for weekends. It teaches responsibility, produces gratitude,
strengthens relationships, and reminds us that we are capable of difficult things
with the Lord's help. We are not trying to raise people who avoid hard work. We
are trying to raise people who find something like joy in it.

---

One of our values says **we work hard and do hard things**, and those words were
chosen carefully. Working hard does not mean staying busy so as to appear
productive, which is a different activity that happens to look similar. It means
giving real effort to whatever God has actually put in front of us.

Sometimes that is school and sometimes it is a job. Often it is caring for a
baby, helping a neighbor, cleaning the kitchen, serving in a calling, or
finishing a project nobody else will ever know about. God appears to draw no line
between important work and ordinary work. All honest work has dignity in it: the
dishes matter, the lawn matters, homework matters, changing a diaper matters, and
getting dinner on the table for seven people matters. Every act of faithful
stewardship becomes an offering when it is done with love.

---

The world celebrates talent. We would rather admire diligence, for the simple
reason that talent is a gift and diligence is a choice. One child learns
something quickly while another has to practice for years, and neither child's
worth has anything to do with which one they are. The Lord has never asked
anybody to compare their gifts with somebody else's; He asks us to be faithful
with the ones we were given.

That is why we celebrate effort as much as achievement in this house. We cheer
for perseverance, honor integrity, and admire the quiet determination that keeps
turning up long after the initial excitement has worn off. It is the least
glamorous virtue and it builds more than any other.

---

There is a particular joy in discovering that you can do a hard thing, and
children stumble onto it before adults remember it. The first bicycle, the first
recital, the first genuinely difficult math assignment, the first long hike, the
first honest apology — each one says the same thing quietly, which is *you are
stronger than you thought you were.*

Part of our job as parents is therefore to resist rescuing our children from
every challenge, which is harder than it sounds and goes against every instinct
we have. Love does not remove the obstacle. Love walks up the hill alongside
somebody while they climb it. We hope this home is a place where difficult things
are expected, supported, and celebrated — not because suffering is good, but
because growth usually costs something. **Keep walking** is the family's answer
on the days when progress is slower than anybody wanted. Some mountains only ever
get climbed one determined step at a time.

---

Work should be done with gratitude, which takes intention, whereas complaining
takes none at all. There will always be chores nobody wants, jobs that feel
repetitive, and responsibilities that arrive at the worst possible moment. It is
worth noticing how many of them are evidence of blessings: laundry means we have
clothes, dishes mean we have food, yard work means we have a home, and helping a
younger sibling means we have a family. Work has a way of revealing the gifts we
had stopped noticing.

Rest belongs in the same picture. The Lord established the Sabbath before He
established nations, and even Jesus withdrew from the crowds to pray and recover.
Neither laziness nor permanent exhaustion is the balance God intends, and this
family is quite capable of both. We hope to work wholeheartedly while leaving
real room for worship, family, laughter, recreation, and quiet. Productivity is
not a measure of anybody's worth here. Our value was settled by Heavenly Father
long before we did anything at all, which means work is something we do because
we are loved rather than something we do to be loved.

---

As our children grow they will find gifts that differ from one another's. Some
may build, some may teach, some may write, design, heal, lead, or serve quietly
somewhere behind the scenes. Whatever honest path the Lord prepares, we hope they
remember that work is never only about making a living; it is about making a
contribution. The question is not only "what career should I choose" but "how can
I bless God's children through the work He has given me to do." That second
question turns employment into discipleship without changing the job.

There will also be seasons when work is discouraging. Projects fail, applications
are rejected, businesses struggle, and goals take years longer than expected.
None of that means God has left. Sometimes He is teaching patience, sometimes
perseverance, sometimes humility, and sometimes compassion for other people
walking the same road. Some blessings come soon and some come late. Faithful work
is never wasted, because even when the results are invisible God is still shaping
the worker, which may be the more important project.

---

We hope the Birches become known as a family that can be counted on: when we give
our word people trust it, when we accept a responsibility we finish it, when a
neighbor needs help we show up, and when a task is difficult we do not
immediately start looking for the way around it. Every honest day's work, noticed
or not, is part of building the kingdom of God — one chore done with love, one
assignment finished with integrity, one promise kept, one difficult conversation
handled with courage. That is how character gets built, and it is how we hope to
live.

> "Men are, that they might have joy."
>
> — 2 Nephi 2:25
