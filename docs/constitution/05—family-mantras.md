# The Birch Family Constitution

# Family Mantras

Every family develops a language of its own. Some phrases survive because they
are funny; these fifteen survive because they turned out to be true, and because
saying six words is sometimes the only teaching a tired parent can manage at
bedtime. They are short on purpose. A mantra that needs explaining every time has
already failed at the one job a mantra has.

Two things are worth understanding before reading them, and the family app makes
the same distinction on its Mantras page. The **mantra** — the short phrase in
bold — is ours. We say it to each other, we have shaped it over years, and it
belongs to this family. The **quotation** beneath it is not ours. Those are the
words of real people, reproduced exactly as they said them, with the talk and the
occasion named so that any Birch can go and read the whole thing. Where a mantra
is our own paraphrase of somebody's idea rather than their words, it says so
plainly. We would rather be scrupulous about this than sound impressive. Putting
words into a prophet's mouth because they sounded about right is not a small
mistake.

These are not rules to memorize. They are invitations to live wisely, love
deeply, and follow Jesus Christ one ordinary day at a time.

## Relationships

### The person comes before the problem.

Broken things can be repaired, spilled milk can be wiped up, and late homework
can still be finished. The person standing in front of us is worth more than
whatever has gone wrong, and they can usually tell within about two seconds which
one we are more concerned about. So we care for the person first and solve the
problem second — in that order, and out loud.

> "Never let a problem to be solved become more important than a person to be
> loved."
>
> — President Thomas S. Monson,
> *Finding Joy in the Journey*, October 2008 general conference

### Say it now.

Kind words are worth most while the person can still hear them. We say "I love
you," "I'm proud of you," "thank you," and "I'm sorry" without waiting for a
better moment, because the better moment has a poor record of arriving. Nobody in
this family has ever regretted saying it one time too many.

> "We will never regret the kind words spoken or the affection shown."
>
> — President Thomas S. Monson,
> *Finding Joy in the Journey*, October 2008 general conference

### Love is the antidote.

When things get tense between two people in this house, the instinct is to win.
Winning has never once fixed it. Charity heals what pride cannot, and charity
here means the specific, unglamorous decision to be kind to the person you are
currently annoyed with.

> "Charity is the antidote to contention."
>
> — President Russell M. Nelson,
> *Peacemakers Needed*, April 2023 general conference

### We are peacemakers, on purpose.

Nobody in this family gets dragged into an argument by accident. Walking away,
softening a voice, listening properly, forgiving, going first with an apology —
every one of those is a choice, available at the time, to the person making it.

> "Contention is a choice. Peacemaking is a choice. You have your agency to
> choose contention or reconciliation."
>
> — President Russell M. Nelson,
> *Peacemakers Needed*, April 2023 general conference

## Perspective

### Find joy in the journey — now.

Not after the house is clean, not after this busy stretch is over, and not next
summer. This unremarkable Tuesday in Rexburg is not the waiting room for the
journey. It is the journey.

> "Find joy in the journey—now."
>
> — President Thomas S. Monson,
> *Finding Joy in the Journey*, October 2008 general conference

### Joy is about where we look.

A hard week is still a hard week, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. But
what we point our hearts and minds at all day long turns out to affect how we
experience that week more than the week itself does.

> "The joy we feel has little to do with the circumstances of our lives and
> everything to do with the focus of our lives."
>
> — President Russell M. Nelson, then President of the Quorum of the Twelve
> Apostles, *Joy and Spiritual Survival*, October 2016 general conference

### Think celestial.

Ask the long question. Not "what do I want right now," but "who am I becoming,
and who do I want to be with forever?" Almost every hard decision gets simpler
once that question is on the table.

> "Mortality is a master class in learning to choose the things of greatest
> eternal import."
>
> — President Russell M. Nelson,
> *Think Celestial!*, October 2023 general conference

### Let God prevail.

When what we want and what He asks pull in different directions, He wins. That
single decision, made once and properly, makes a hundred smaller ones
straightforward.

> "When your greatest desire is to let God prevail, to be part of Israel, so many
> decisions become easier."
>
> — President Russell M. Nelson,
> *Let God Prevail*, October 2020 general conference

## Growth

### Keep walking.

This is the Birch family answer to a hard thing. Not "cheer up," which is
useless, and not "it doesn't matter," which is untrue. Just keep going. We say it
to each other on the long winter days.

> "Don't give up, boy. Don't you quit. You keep walking. You keep trying."
>
> — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland,
> *"An High Priest of Good Things to Come"*, October 1999 general conference

### Some blessings come late. They still come.

We do not get to choose God's timing, and some of the answers this family is
waiting on have been a long time coming. We do get to keep believing they are on
their way.

> "Some blessings come soon, some come late, and some don't come until heaven;
> but for those who embrace the gospel of Jesus Christ, they come."
>
> — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland,
> *"An High Priest of Good Things to Come"*, October 1999 general conference

### Learn from it. Don't live in it.

Yesterday's mistake is a teacher, not an address. We take the lesson, repent,
leave the shame behind — it was never useful — and face forward.

> "The past is to be learned from but not lived in."
>
> — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland,
> *Remember Lot's Wife*, Brigham Young University devotional, 13 January 2009

### We start again today.

Repentance is not a punishment in this house. It is the fresh start, and it is
available every single morning. Nobody here is stuck being the version of
themselves they were yesterday.

> "Daily repentance is the pathway to purity, and purity brings power."
>
> — President Russell M. Nelson,
> *We Can Do Better and Be Better*, April 2019 general conference

## Service

### Be somebody's angel today.

Sitting by the new kid at lunch. Shoveling the neighbor's walk before they wake
up. Most of the help God sends to people arrives wearing boots and answering to
an ordinary name.

> "But when we speak of those who are instruments in the hand of God, we are
> reminded that not all angels are from the other side of the veil. Some of them
> we walk with and talk with—here, now, every day."
>
> — Elder Jeffrey R. Holland,
> *The Ministry of Angels*, October 2008 general conference

### Bless the one.

You cannot fix everything for everyone, and trying is a reliable route to doing
nothing at all. You can notice one person today and do one real thing for them.
That is how a family, a ward, and a town actually get better.

> "When you bless the one, you bless the whole."
>
> — an unnamed bishop, quoted by Sister Kristin M. Yee in
> *Ministering—"That Ye Love One Another; as I Have Loved You"*,
> April 2026 general conference

### We are His hands.

When somebody in Rexburg is praying for help, the answer is very often just a
person who decided to show up. Around here, that is the job.

> "We are the Savior's hands."
>
> — Sister Kristin M. Yee,
> *Ministering—"That Ye Love One Another; as I Have Loved You"*,
> April 2026 general conference

---

These fifteen are woven through the ordinary days of this family, and one of them
comes up on the app's home screen every morning. We repeat them not because they
are catchy but because they make us pause at the moment when pausing is the whole
difficulty. Over time we hope they stop being sayings and become habits, and that
the habits quietly become character.

> "By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to
> another."
>
> — John 13:35
