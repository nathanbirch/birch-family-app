# The Birch Family Constitution

## Preface

> "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord."
>
> — Joshua 24:15

Every family has a culture. Some families build theirs on purpose and some drift
into one by accident, but nobody escapes having one. The way a house sounds on a
Tuesday evening, what gets laughed at, what gets apologized for, who gets
listened to — all of it is being decided whether or not anyone is paying
attention. We would rather pay attention.

This is not a book of rules. It is a description of who we are trying to become,
written down while we are still in the middle of trying. Long before we care
about grades, careers, talent, money, or recognition, we care about becoming
disciples of Jesus Christ, and everything in these pages grows out of that one
commitment.

We will not live up to it. We will be impatient, say things we wish we had not
said, forget on Thursday what mattered on Sunday, and need forgiveness from each
other more often than any of us would like to admit. That is expected, and it is
written here deliberately, so that no future Birch reads this book and concludes
that the people who wrote it had it all figured out. Perfection is not the goal
of this family. Discipleship is.

We believe Jesus Christ is the Son of God, that He lives, and that His Atonement
makes real change possible. Because of Him, failure is never final, repentance is
never shameful, and tomorrow can genuinely be better than today. That belief is
the reason a book like this one is worth writing at all; without it, a family
constitution would only be a longer list of ways to fall short.

So this document is not meant to be used as a weapon. It is not something we
quote at each other to win an argument, and it is not a ruler for measuring which
Birch is the good one. It is a compass. When we lose our way, when we disagree,
when life gets busy or frightening or confusing, we come back here and remember
which direction we chose.

Some of what follows came from scripture and some from prophets and apostles. A
good deal of it we learned the hard way, through years of ordinary family life —
some of it through tears, some through laughter, and a surprising amount through
both at once. Certain phrases turn up again and again in these pages because they
turn up again and again in this house: *Love like Jesus. Think celestial. The
person comes before the problem. Keep walking. Find joy in the journey. Bless the
one.* They are more than sayings. Each one is an invitation, and each one points
toward the Savior.

Our hope is not that our children memorize these words. Our hope is that they
become the kind of people who would have arrived at them on their own. If
somebody someday asks what it means to be a Birch, we hope the answer has very
little to do with our surname and almost everything to do with character. A Birch
tells the truth and keeps promises, works hard and does hard things, and chooses
kindness over being right. A Birch serves quietly, apologizes sincerely, and
forgives generously. A Birch keeps learning, keeps repenting, and leaves every
person and every place a little better than they found it. Most of all, a Birch
follows Jesus Christ.

This is also a gift to people we may never meet. One day our children will have
children, and those children may wonder what mattered to the family they came
from. When that day arrives, we hope they will not remember us because we were
successful. We hope they remember that we tried — tried to love each other, to
laugh often, to keep our covenants, to serve our neighbors, and to build a home
where repentance was joyful, forgiveness was quick, kindness was ordinary, and
Jesus Christ was always welcome at the table. We were trying to make this house
feel a little more like heaven. If these pages help even one future Birch become
slightly more patient, courageous, honest, or Christlike, they will have done
everything we wanted them to do.

To every Birch who reads this, whether you are six years old or ninety-six, there
is one thing here that matters more than all the rest, and we would rather say it
at the beginning than bury it at the end.

**Your worth has never depended on your performance.**

You are, first and forever, a beloved child of Heavenly Parents. Nothing in this
book changes that, nothing you do can increase it, and nothing you do can spend
it. Everything else written here is simply our family's attempt to answer that
love with the way we live.

May our home always be a place where truth is spoken kindly, forgiveness comes
quickly, laughter is abundant, work is honorable, learning never stops, and the
Savior would be comfortable sitting down to dinner. That is the family we are
trying to build, and with His help, we will.
