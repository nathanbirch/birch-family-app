# The Birch Family Constitution

# Mission and Vision

> "Happiness in family life is most likely to be achieved when founded upon the
> teachings of the Lord Jesus Christ."
>
> — The Family: A Proclamation to the World

## Our mission

The Birch family exists to help one another become faithful disciples of Jesus
Christ. That is the purpose, stated as plainly as we know how to state it, and
everything else we do — the work, the schooling, the traditions, the recreation,
the service, the ordinary logistics of getting seven people fed and out the door
— flows from it or else has quietly stopped mattering.

We believe families are part of Heavenly Father's plan, and that a home is more
than a place to eat and sleep. It is a place to become. Within these walls we
hope each person feels known, loved, safe, and genuinely challenged to grow —
which are not the same thing and cannot substitute for one another. A home that
is safe but never challenging raises comfortable people; a home that challenges
without safety raises anxious ones. We are aiming at both.

The distinction we care about most is between control and help. It would be
possible to run a household that produces obedient children and teaches them
nothing about why. Our mission is not to control one another. It is to help one
another become — which is slower, less tidy, and the only version worth the
years it takes.

## Our vision

We hope to become a family whose presence makes a place a little brighter. When
people spend an evening with the Birches, we hope they leave feeling welcomed,
encouraged, valued, and somewhat closer to Jesus Christ than when they arrived.
We hope this is known as a house where neighbors get invited in, where friends
find belonging, where burdens get lighter, and where nobody has to pretend to be
doing better than they are.

Our children should grow up understanding that home is the safest place in the
world to fail, to repent, to ask an uncomfortable question, and to begin again.
If they cannot bring us the worst thing they have done, we have built the wrong
house.

We would rather be known for quiet goodness than for loud recognition. Not
because attention is wicked, but because the habit of seeking it tends to
crowd out the habit of noticing other people, and noticing other people is most
of the job.

## How we measure success

The world measures success by wealth, recognition, achievement, influence,
titles, and popularity. Those can all be good gifts, honestly earned and
honorably used, and we have no interest in pretending otherwise. They are simply
not what we are keeping score with.

For this family, success is becoming more like Jesus Christ. It is telling the
truth when hiding would be easier, choosing kindness when being right would be
more satisfying, and forgiving when resentment feels entirely justified. It is
keeping promises, serving without being asked, working diligently at things
nobody will notice, repenting quickly, and still learning something new at
eighty. It is blessing the one. If we become those kinds of people, we will
consider our lives successful no matter what any other scoreboard says.

## The family we hope to become

We hope to become the family that notices the lonely child before anyone else
does and brings dinner before being asked. The family that keeps its promises,
apologizes sincerely, laughs often, welcomes the neighbors, chooses peace over
contention, works hard without complaining about it, and never quite finishes
learning. We hope this is the home where people feel safe enough to be honest,
where children know they are loved even when they have failed, and where every
person in it is being encouraged toward the Savior a little at a time.

If future generations remember anything about us, we hope it is not what we
owned or what we accomplished, but that we loved deeply, served generously, kept
our covenants, and left people and places better than we found them. That is the
Birch family we are striving to become, one choice and one ordinary day at a
time.
