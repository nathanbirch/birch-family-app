# The Birch Family Constitution

# Family Values

Values are the principles that guide our choices, and they shape the atmosphere
of a home long before anybody says them out loud. They show up in how we respond
when life is peaceful and, more revealingly, in how we respond when it is not.
Over enough years they stop being things a family believes and become simply how
that family is.

We chose these eight because we believe they reflect the teachings of Jesus
Christ and describe the people we hope to become. We will not live them
perfectly. We will return to them often, which is the more important half.

---

## 1. Jesus Christ is first. Always.

He is the foundation of this home. We seek to follow Him ahead of popularity,
comfort, success, and the settled opinions of the world — including the settled
opinions we ourselves held last year. When we are genuinely unsure what to do,
His teachings are the first place we look, not the last place we check.

## 2. We love and serve each other.

This family is a gift from Heavenly Father rather than an accident of address,
and we choose to care for one another accordingly. We celebrate together, carry
each other's burdens, forgive generously, and look for the chance to help before
being asked. Whatever we become for the world, we become at home first. Service
starts here.

## 3. We love our neighbors.

Kindness does not stop at the front door. We want to be the household that
notices who is lonely, discouraged, overlooked, or quietly struggling, and then
actually does something about it. We believe small acts of goodness change lives
more reliably than large intentions do, and we hope every community we live in is
better for our having been in it.

## 4. We work hard and do hard things.

Anything worth having is usually difficult, and we are not afraid of effort,
responsibility, or the long unglamorous middle of a project. Work builds
character in a way that nothing else quite manages, and difficult experiences are
frequently where faith and resilience turn out to have been growing. We are not
looking for the easy path. We are looking for the worthwhile one.

## 5. We tell the truth and keep our promises.

Trust is built one honest choice at a time and spent much faster than it
accumulates. We speak truthfully, admit mistakes without decorating them, honor
our commitments, and try to be people whose word does not need checking.
Integrity is doing the right thing when there is no possibility of being caught.

## 6. We choose kindness over being right.

There are arguments a person can win and lose something far more valuable in the
process. Relationships matter more than pride here. We listen, try to understand,
and answer gently — while still being honest and still having courage, because
kindness that cannot tell the truth is not kindness, only avoidance. We measure
ourselves not by whether we won but by whether we loved well.

## 7. We keep learning, growing, and repenting.

Growth is one of God's better gifts, and it requires being correctable. We
welcome learning, receive correction with as much humility as we can find on the
day, and remember that repentance is a joyful thing because of Jesus Christ.
Yesterday does not get to decide who we are. With the Savior's help, anybody in
this house can begin again this afternoon.

## 8. We leave every person and place better than we found it.

Whether we are visiting a friend's home, sitting in a church meeting, finishing a
project, or just passing through, we hope things are a little better for our
having been there. We want people to feel encouraged after being with us and
places to be tidier, kinder, and more welcoming because we cared enough to
bother. It is one of the simplest ways available to love like Jesus.

---

These are not a checklist to complete or a standard for comparing ourselves with
other families. They are a direction we have agreed to walk together. When we
lose our way we come back to them; when things go well we give thanks to God;
and when we fall short we remember that discipleship has never been about
avoiding the stumble. It is about getting up again through the grace of Jesus
Christ, as many times as that takes.

> "By small and simple things are great things brought to pass."
>
> — Alma 37:6
