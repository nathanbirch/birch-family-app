# Accessibility

## Every scene has a text equivalent

Each seating card carries a screen-reader-only description of exactly the
assignments the picture shows:

> Nathan is in Parent Seat 1, on the left bench, top.
> Sarah is in Parent Seat 2, on the right bench, top.
> Hannah is in Child Seat 1, on the left bench, middle.
> …

These are generated by `lib/seating-summary.ts` from the *same* assignment data
the graphics use, never written by hand — so they cannot drift out of sync. A
test asserts every visible name appears in the description, in both scenes.

The photographs themselves are `aria-hidden` with empty `alt`: they are
decoration, and the description carries the meaning.

## Not colour alone

- Every avatar shows the person's **name as text** beside it, so nobody has to
  recognise a colour.
- Every avatar carries a **first-initial badge** as a second non-colour cue.
- The theme picker marks the active theme with a **checkmark** and
  `aria-checked`, not just a highlight.
- The swap button reports its state through **`aria-pressed`** and a half-turn
  of its arrows.

## Contrast

All ten themes clear **4.5:1** for body text, muted text and text-on-primary.
The worst ratio across the set is 4.93:1. See [Themes](themes.md).

## Keyboard and touch

- Every control is at least **44px** in its smallest dimension.
- A visible, themed focus ring is applied via `:focus-visible` — 3px, offset,
  in the theme's focus colour.
- The theme picker closes on **Escape** and returns focus to its trigger.
- Clicking outside the picker closes it without stealing focus.

## Motion

Everything is an entrance or a transition. Nothing loops, and nothing moves
once the three-second arrival settles — no bouncing avatars.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, all of it is switched off: the arrival,
the swap glide and arc, the card entrances, and the theme transition. People
are simply in their seats already.

## Structure

- One `<h1>` (the app name), `<h2>` per card, `<h3>` inside the descriptions.
- The status panel uses a `<dl>` for its facts and `<time datetime>` for every
  date, so dates are machine-readable as well as friendly.
- The rotation status card is labelled by a heading that is visually hidden but
  present for screen readers.

## Known limits

- The scene photographs are not described beyond "decorative". Someone using a
  screen reader gets the seating, not the décor — which is the useful part.
- There is one frame on first load where the parent-swap preference has not yet
  been applied, because it can only be read on the client. It is invisible in
  practice: the avatars are still off-screen at their doorways at that point.
